# 0.2.9 plan: Panopticon NAC, closing the loop

**Status:** Q1–Q5 built; released as 0.2.9 (Q6). Lab checks still open.

0.2.8 made the admission decision richer: who logged in (AD groups), what
an admitted device may reach (roles), what the other arsenals know about it
(security signals at login), and when the NAS asks again (Session-Timeout).
Three gaps remain in how that decision behaves once a device is on the
network:

- **A decision is made only at login.** CoA already re-authenticates a
  session when a device's trust changes, its port or device is quarantined
  or released, or a registration is decided
  (`panopticon_coa::spawn_reauthorize_mac` / `spawn_reauthorize_port`).
  Nothing does it when a **security signal** starts or clears (a posture
  run, a Thanatos event opened or resolved, a Scourge alert) or when the
  RADIUS policy itself changes. A machine compromised mid-session keeps its
  VLAN until the Session-Timeout or a reconnect; a fixed one stays in
  quarantine as long.
- **Nobody can see why.** Every decision's reasons (trust, enforcement,
  signal, group rule, role, timeout) are in the server log only
  (`RADIUS MAB decision`, `FreeRADIUS 802.1X decision`); sessions don't
  record what they were given. The policy engine's *Simulate* mode has the
  same problem.
- **Two cheap detections are missing**: a rogue DHCP server, and a device
  plugged in between SNMP polls.

0.2.9 closes those, in that order, after a test harness that every later
phase uses.

## Before anything: the lab checks

Every feature since 0.2.7 has been tested against real FreeRADIUS, a real
Samba AD DC and real MariaDB, but **no real switch, AP or Windows DC**.
Vendor quirks are most of PacketFence's head start, and what these turn up
goes into 0.2.9 ahead of any phase below.

- [ ] CoA (Disconnect and Cisco reauthenticate) on a Cisco switch and an Aruba controller/switch (0.2.7 N2).
- [ ] MAB, and EAP-TLS through FreeRADIUS, on a real switch (0.2.7 N4).
- [ ] PEAP with a Windows DC: user and computer logins, `AD_REQUIRE_GROUP`, Credential Guard behaviour (0.2.8).
- [ ] VLAN by AD group with nested groups on a Windows DC (P1).
- [ ] A Cisco named ACL (`Filter-Id = QUARANTINE.in`) and an Aruba role applied to a quarantined device (P2).
- [ ] A host whose posture drops below the threshold lands in quarantine at its next 802.1X login, and leaves it after remediation (P3).
- [ ] A NAS with CoA off picks up a trust change at the Session-Timeout (P4).
- [ ] The registration portal's captive sign-in on iOS and Android (0.2.7 N5).

## Order of work

One phase at a time on `Dev`, each finished and checked before the next.

| # | Phase | Size | Agent change? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Q1 | RADIUS end-to-end test harness; housekeeping | Small | No |
| Q2 | Decision records and "why" | Medium | No |
| Q3 | Re-evaluate open sessions (signals and policy changes reach live sessions) | Medium | No |
| Q4 | Rogue DHCP server detection (B7) | Small | No |
| Q5 | SNMP traps (B1) | Medium | No |
| Q6 | Release: version, CHANGELOG, `Dev` → `main`, tag | Small | - |

Q2 comes before Q3 because re-evaluating a session means comparing against
what it was given, which Q2 stores. Q5 is the stretch phase: if it doesn't
fit, it moves to 0.2.10 without holding up the release.

As in 0.2.8, everything new is off until configured: with the new settings
untouched, every RADIUS decision and every CoA is exactly what 0.2.8 does.

## Q2. Decision records and "why"

### Decisions to make

| Question | Leaning |
| --- | --- |
| What's stored? | One row per RADIUS decision (migration, `panopticon_radius_decisions`): time, MAC, NAS client and address, front end (embedded / FreeRADIUS) and admission (MAB / 802.1X), identity (the real login for PEAP), the outcome (accept / reject), VLAN, role, Session-Timeout, and the reasons: trust state, enforcement in effect, security signal (`LoginDecision::signal_label`), AD group rule and the login's matched groups. |
| How does a session find its decision? | Access-Accept comes before accounting Start, so by MAC and NAS address: a session's decision is the latest one for its MAC from its NAS at or before `started_at` (and, for re-authentications, the latest overall). No foreign key: decisions for rejected logins have no session at all. |
| What if the write fails? | The login is decided anyway; the failure is logged. A record is for people, never part of the decision. |
| How big does it get? | Every login and every Session-Timeout re-authentication writes a row. Kept for `panopticon.radius_decision_retention_days` (default 30), pruned by the existing Panopticon retention sweep; indexed on (MAC, time) and (time). |
| Where is it shown? | The device page: its last decisions with reasons ("quarantine: Cadavault posture score 40 (below 60)"). The RADIUS page's sessions table: each session's current decision. A **Decisions** list on the RADIUS page, filterable by MAC, NAS and outcome. |
| "Test a login"? | A form on the RADIUS page (MAC, NAS client, MAB or 802.1X, optional AD groups) that runs `decide_for_login` and shows the decision and reasons **without** recording it or touching the NAS. `network.view` is enough: it changes nothing. |
| Policy engine *Simulate* (B6)? | The same idea for the policy engine: in Simulate mode, each "would have enforced" match is stored (rule, device, port or RADIUS path, reason) and shown on the policy page instead of only logged. Same retention. |

### Tasks

- [x] Migration 0064: `panopticon_radius_decisions` and `panopticon_policy_simulations` (one row per rule and device, upserted each sweep with a count).
- [x] `decide_explained`: every decision carries what it was based on ("trusted device", "security signal: quarantine (otherwise: trusted device)", "untrusted device: quarantine (by role)", the fail-closed rejects, ", by AD group rule"); `LoginDecision` keeps the reason and the login's AD groups.
- [x] Recorded by every caller (embedded server, FreeRADIUS `mab` and both `post-auth` paths) in the background: the reply never waits on the write, and a failed write is only logged. The role recorded is the one actually sent. The NAS address is the one accounting files the session under (NAS-IP-Address, else the source), so sessions find their decision. "Test a login" simply doesn't record.
- [x] Repo: insert (values cut to their columns, never rejected), filtered list, decisions for a set of MACs; `abyssal_core::decision_for_session` picks a session's decision (latest from shortly before it started until it stopped, its own NAS first). Retention: `panopticon.radius_decision_retention_days` (default 30, 0 = forever) on the RADIUS page, pruned every six hours (`panopticon_radius_retention` task), simulations too.
- [x] UI: **Decisions** page (`/arsenals/panopticon/radius/decisions`, `network.view`) with MAC / NAS / outcome filters and **test a login**; a Decision column in the RADIUS sessions table; inventory MACs link to their decisions (there's no per-device page); the policy page's **Simulated matches** table with Clear (`network.nac`, audited).
- [x] Tests: reasons for each kind of outcome, what the record holds (the role sent, groups, front end), `decision_for_session`, summaries; the harness (now 68 checks) checks the rows written by both front ends with signal, identity, AD groups and group rule, the Decisions page and its filter, test-a-login (and that it isn't recorded), the sessions column, and Simulate mode across two starts (a skipped match turning into a RADIUS one when the device gets a session, the count going up, the policy page, Clear).

Found on the way: `repo::panopticon_radius::prune_sessions_older_than` had no caller, so accounted sessions were never pruned. Fixed in Q3.

## Q3. Re-evaluate open sessions

### Decisions to make

| Question | Leaning |
| --- | --- |
| What triggers it? | A sweep every `panopticon.radius_reevaluate_seconds` (default 60) while `panopticon.radius_reevaluate_enabled` is on (**off by default**: turning on a login signal shouldn't disconnect anyone until the admin also asks for live re-evaluation). It catches every cause at once (signals starting or clearing, RADIUS settings, roles, group rules, timeouts) instead of a hook per cause. Existing CoA hooks (trust, enforcement, registration) stay as they are, for speed. |
| What does it compare? | For each open accounted session (`stopped_at IS NULL`, seen within the stale-session window), the decision it was given (Q2) against `decide_for_login` now, with the **same AD groups** the login brought (stored in Q2's record, since a CoA'd re-login through FreeRADIUS brings them again but the sweep itself can't ask AD). Different outcome, VLAN or role: reauthorize. A different Session-Timeout alone doesn't count. |
| How? | The existing CoA path (`panopticon_coa::reauthorize_session`), per the NAS client's `coa_mode`. A NAS with CoA off is skipped: P4's Session-Timeout covers it. |
| Guards | Per session: at most one CoA per `panopticon.radius_reevaluate_cooldown_minutes` (default 15), so a NAK or a NAS that ignores CoA isn't hammered. Per sweep: at most `panopticon.radius_reevaluate_max_per_sweep` (default 25) CoAs, the rest next time, so a policy change doesn't disconnect the whole floor at once. The control plane's own server is never touched (as in P3). |
| Cost | One `decide_for_login` per open session per sweep, about six small queries each. Fine for hundreds of sessions; if it's ever thousands, read the signals in bulk once per sweep as the policy engine does (`Signals`). Settled when built, with a measurement. |
| Audited? | Each CoA is already audited (`NETWORK_RADIUS_REAUTHORIZED`); the reason ("re-evaluation: signal quarantine: ...") goes in its metadata. |

### Tasks

- [x] Settings `panopticon.radius_reevaluate_{enabled,seconds,cooldown_minutes,max_per_sweep}` (off; 60 s, 10–3600; 15 min, 1–1440; 25, 1–500) in a "Live re-evaluation (CoA)" fieldset on the RADIUS page, checked on save (absent numbers keep their values) and clamped again when read.
- [x] Prune accounted sessions: the retention sweep now also deletes sessions not seen for the decision retention (stopped, or open on a NAS that never sent Stop). Open sessions not seen for 24 hours aren't re-evaluated.
- [x] The sweep (`panopticon_reevaluate`): wakes every 10 s and runs when enabled and its interval has passed; open sessions least recently touched first; skips a NAS client with CoA off (or none), a session in cooldown (any CoA, `last_coa_at`), one with no recorded decision; re-runs `decide_for_login` with the recorded admission and AD groups through the session's own NAS client; compares outcome, VLAN and role sent (not Session-Timeout); never the control plane (a lookup error counts as control plane); at most N per sweep, the rest counted as deferred. The CoA's audit `why` reads "re-evaluation: accept, VLAN 10, role EMPLOYEE.in -> accept, VLAN 999, role QUARANTINE.in (security signal: quarantine (...))". Task health: `panopticon_radius_reevaluate`.
- [x] Tests: what counts as a change, the cooldown, the settings' bounds. The cap and the CoA-off skip are in the sweep's loop, covered by reading, not by a test.
- [x] Harness (Q1, now 79 checks): a stand-in NAS (`deploy/test/fake_nas.py`, signed Disconnect-/CoA-ACKs). A posture drop on a session's host gets a Disconnect within a sweep, ACKed, the session closed and the CoA audited with why; an unchanged session and the control plane's (whose decision did change, as its debug log shows) are left alone; after the device re-authenticates, its new session isn't re-authorized again (exactly one request in all).
- [ ] Lab: a session on a switch with CoA on leaves quarantine within a minute of a posture fix, without waiting for the Session-Timeout.

## Q4. Rogue DHCP server detection (B7)

### Decisions to make

| Question | Leaning |
| --- | --- |
| How is a DHCP server seen? | Two ways, both from the existing capture (`panopticon_arp`): **option 54** (server identifier) in client REQUESTs, which the capture already parses (`abyssal_portal_net::dhcp::Request::server_id`) and which also arrives through relays (`ip helper-address`), so it shows which server clients actually accepted; and **OFFER/ACK** packets to UDP 68 on the captured segment, which add the server's MAC but are local only. |
| What's "allowed"? | `panopticon.dhcp_allowed_servers`, a list of addresses and CIDRs. **Empty = detection off.** The page lists every server seen (address, MAC when known, first/last seen, how many clients chose it), so the list can be built from what's there. |
| What happens on a rogue? | An alert through the existing notification channel and an audit event, once per server per day; the server shows red on the page. And a new **policy trigger**, "rogue DHCP server", so the policy engine can quarantine the device that answered (matched by the source MAC of its own unrelayed reply, never by a named address; through RADIUS or its port) with every existing safety (mode, uplink guard, control-plane guard). No automatic action without a policy rule. |
| False positives | A legitimate server not yet listed (fix: list it); a client renewing with a stale server id after a server move (it's only a rogue if clients keep choosing it). Panopticon's own registration DHCP server is always allowed. |

### Tasks

- [x] Capture: a REQUEST's option 54 (REQUESTs only, not DISCOVERs) and server replies, UDP 67 → 68, OFFER/ACK/NAK (`abyssal_portal_net::dhcp::parse_server_reply`, the same bounds-checked style as the request parser): the server is option 54, else the packet's source; its MAC is the Ethernet source only for an unrelayed reply (a relayed one comes from the relay).
- [x] Migration 0065: `panopticon_dhcp_servers` (address, MAC, first/last seen, replies, chosen by clients, last client, last alerted); setting `panopticon.dhcp_allowed_servers`; core `parse_allowed_dhcp_servers` / `is_rogue_dhcp_server` (a list that doesn't parse is refused on save, and counts as detection off if it somehow gets stored).
- [x] Alert through the rogue-device recipients (`panopticon.rogue_alert_recipients`) and the audit log (`NETWORK_ROGUE_DHCP_SERVER`), once a day per server and at most 10 an hour in all; the `rogue_dhcp` policy trigger (within the signal window, through every existing guard), with tests.
- [x] **The trigger matches by MAC, never by address** (found by a security review): every DHCP field is the sender's to write, so any device could name any address as a server (option 54, or a forged reply) and get that address's device quarantined. Enforcement goes by the Ethernet source of an unrelayed reply the rogue actually sent. A server only named by clients, or seen through a relay, is shown and alerted on as *unconfirmed* and never enforced. Known limit: one MAC is kept per server address (the latest unrelayed reply's), and a device that spoofs another's MAC on the segment can still implicate it, as with any L2 evidence.
- [x] DHCP page section: servers seen, allowed or rogue (or "detection off"), when last alerted; the allowed list; **Allow** (adds the address) and **Forget** per server (`network.manage`, audited).
- [x] Harness (now 91 checks): the allowed list and its validation, rogue/allowed/unconfirmed on the page, Allow, Forget, and the `rogue_dhcp` rule in Simulate matching the rogue's device by the MAC it answered from (through RADIUS, with the reason), and neither the allowed server's device nor a device whose address was only named as a server. The capture itself needs `CAP_NET_RAW`, which the harness doesn't run the app with: the servers it would have seen are seeded, and the capture → record → alert path is covered by unit tests of its parts (frame parsing, the rogue check, once-a-day), not end to end.
- [ ] Lab: a second DHCP server (a laptop running dnsmasq) on a test VLAN is flagged within a minute, and the alert arrives.

## Q5. SNMP traps (B1)

Discovery and port location come from SNMP polls (`panopticon_snmp`), so a
device plugged in between polls is invisible until the next one, and
policies like "new unknown device" wait for it too.

### Decisions to make

| Question | Leaning |
| --- | --- |
| What's received? | `linkUp` / `linkDown` (`1.3.6.1.6.3.1.1.5.4` / `.3`) from any switch, and Cisco **MAC-notification** (`cmnMacChangedNotification`) where configured, which names the MAC and port directly. Other vendors' MAC traps later, as the lab finds them. |
| Who is believed? | Only switches Panopticon already polls (by source address), and only with the switch's own SNMP community (v1/v2c). SNMPv3 traps (USM, the switch's engine ID) are left for later unless `snmp2` makes them simple; a v3-only switch keeps being polled as before. |
| What happens? | A trap schedules an immediate poll of that switch (its FDB and the port), debounced: at most one trap-triggered poll per switch per `panopticon.snmp_trap_min_interval_seconds` (default 10). A MAC-notification trap updates the device's location at once. Devices found or moved by it are run through the policy engine right away instead of at its next sweep. |
| Listener | UDP 162 (or `panopticon.snmp_trap_port`), started when `panopticon.snmp_traps_enabled` is on (default off). The container already has `CAP_NET_BIND_SERVICE` (the portal). Behind Docker's bridge network, publish UDP 162. |
| Load | A flapping port sends a trap storm: the debounce bounds the polls, and traps from unknown sources are dropped without parsing past the header. |

### Tasks

- [x] Trap listener (`panopticon_snmp_traps`): `snmp2` decodes v1 and v2c traps (no BER reader needed); the source is checked against the enabled v1/v2c switches (a list cached 30 s) **before** the packet is parsed, then the switch's own community, in constant time. v3 traps are dropped (B11).
- [x] linkUp / linkDown (v2c `snmpTrapOID`, or v1 generic 2/3) with their ifIndex, and Cisco `cmnMacChangedNotification` (v2c, or v1 enterprise `...9.9.215.2` specific 1) with its packed MAC changes (operation, VLAN, MAC, bridge port). Any other trap from a switch is recorded, without a poll.
- [x] A link or MAC trap schedules a poll of that switch, debounced: the first at once, then at most one more at the end of `panopticon.snmp_trap_min_interval_seconds` (default 10) however many arrive. After the poll, a policy pass (`run_policy_pass_now`; the sweep and trap passes now share a lock, so two passes never act on a device at once). A MAC-notification trap doesn't update the location itself (its bridge port isn't an ifIndex): the poll does, seconds later.
- [x] Settings `panopticon.snmp_traps_enabled` / `snmp_trap_port` (162) / `snmp_trap_min_interval_seconds` on the switches page (read at startup), with the Cisco config to add; migration 0066: each switch's last accepted trap, shown in a new column.
- [x] Tests: v2c and v1 link traps, Cisco MAC notifications both ways, other traps, truncation at every length, the constant-time compare, the debounce (a burst = two polls). Harness (now 105 checks, `deploy/test/send_trap.py`): a linkDown recorded and polled at once, then a policy pass; the wrong community and a non-switch source (from a container on Docker's bridge) dropped and not recorded; a burst of 5 traps = 2 polls; a MAC notification recorded and shown on the page.
- [ ] Lab: a device plugged into a Cisco access port appears (and is policy-checked) within seconds.
