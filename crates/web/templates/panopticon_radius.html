Quarantine reuses the enforcement quarantine VLAN ({% if quarantine_vlan > 0 %}{{ quarantine_vlan }}{% else %}unset — untrusted will be rejected{% endif %}). Registration portal sends unknown devices to the registration VLAN until a guest registration for them is approved, then to the guest VLAN (Registration portal).
Roles (ACLs / user roles)

The name of an ACL or user role already defined on the switch or controller, sent with each Accept to NAS clients that have a role attribute (below): Cisco named ACLs as NAME.in through Filter-Id, Aruba user roles through Aruba-User-Role. Empty = no role. Letters, digits, _ - . :. A quarantine role also lets quarantine work without a quarantine VLAN, on NAS clients that apply roles: a quarantined device then keeps its VLAN, restricted by the role (for example to reach WSUS and your AV servers). Elsewhere, quarantine without a VLAN still rejects.

Re-authentication (Session-Timeout)

Minutes until the switch or AP asks again, sent as Session-Timeout with Termination-Action = RADIUS-Request: the NAS re-authenticates the session without dropping the link, and the device gets whatever the policy says by then. That's how a trust change, a lifted quarantine or a fixed posture reaches NAS clients without CoA. 0 = none (the NAS keeps the decision until the device reconnects); otherwise {{ timeout_min_minutes }}–{{ timeout_max_minutes }}. Suggested: quarantine and registration 5–15, trusted 480–1440. Some NAS clients only honour it with re-authentication enabled on the port (Cisco: authentication periodic and authentication timer reauthenticate server).

Live re-evaluation (CoA)

Every interval, each open session's decision is worked out again; where the outcome, VLAN or role would change (a security signal started or cleared, a VLAN, role or AD group rule changed), the session is re-authorized through its NAS client's CoA setting, so the change applies now instead of at the next login or Session-Timeout. NAS clients with CoA off are left to the Session-Timeout. A session re-authorized in the last cooldown minutes isn't touched again, at most the given number are re-authorized per sweep (the rest next time), and this server itself never is. Trust changes, enforcement and guest approvals already re-authorize at once, with or without this.

Every login and re-authentication is recorded with what it was based on (Decisions). Also how long the policy engine's simulated matches are kept.

Unknown = Accept is the safe default: turning RADIUS on gives you identity and accounting visibility without locking anyone out. Classify your fleet (Trusted / Untrusted) and then tighten the unknown action to Guest or Reject to actually enforce admission.

The NAS must accept dynamic authorization from this server's address with the same shared secret (Cisco: aaa server radius dynamic-author), on UDP port 3799 unless you set another.

{% call macros::icon("shield-check") %} Security signals at login

Checked at every RADIUS login (MAB, EAP-TLS, PEAP), for devices linked to a managed host (or, for Scourge, by the device's address): the same signals as the policy engine's triggers, but a failing device never reaches its normal VLAN in the first place. A signal only tightens: it turns an admitting decision into quarantine (the quarantine VLAN and role, as for an untrusted device) or a reject. Port and device enforcement still come first, and the control plane's own server is never restricted. Events and alerts count for the last {{ signal_window_minutes }} minutes (the policy engine's signal window). A device that's fixed leaves quarantine at its next login (re-authenticate its session below, or wait for the switch's re-authentication timer).

{% call macros::icon("user") %} VLAN by AD group

For 802.1X logins checked against Active Directory (PEAP through FreeRADIUS, deploy/freeradius/README.md): the first rule, in order, whose group the user or computer is in chooses the VLAN, the role, or both (a blank VLAN keeps the device's VLAN; a blank role keeps the role set above). Groups are DOMAIN\Group, or just Group for that name in any domain, and nested membership counts. A rule only chooses the VLAN of a device that would be admitted anyway: a quarantined or disabled port or device, an Untrusted device, and an unknown device under Reject are treated as above. MAB and EAP-TLS logins don't use these rules. Changes apply at each device's next login (or Reauthorize a session below).
